# Lines & links

Every line on the board answers one question: **how are these two things
related?** The answer is written along the line, and its colour tells you who
said so:

| Colour | Who said so | Can you change it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Grey** | The platform found it in your data: lineage, references, duplicates | No: it is a fact about the data |
| **Amber** (or red) | Someone drew it on this board | Yes: edit, promote or delete it |
| **Green, red or grey** with a glyph | A hypothesis's stance on a piece of evidence | Yes: change or remove the stance |

Dashed ghost lines in other colours belong to **Show connections**; they are
covered in [Connections & neighbours](/investigations/cases/connections/).

## Relations the platform found

When sources are scanned, Classifyre works out how assets relate: a file was
produced from a database table, a document refers to a spreadsheet, two files
hold the same content. On the board these are thin grey lines with their type
written along them:

<LineKey only="found" />

The words along the line are the relation's own name, for example:

| You may read | Kind | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `TRANSFORM`, `COPY` | Lineage | The data flowed from one asset into the other; the arrow shows the direction |
| `REFERENCES`, `ATTACHED_TO`, `links_to` | Link | One asset mentions, attaches or links to the other |
| `identical_content`, `likely_duplicate` | Duplicate | The same content, found in two places |
| `related` | Similar | The contents overlap a lot: near neighbours |
| `contains` | | From an asset to each of its findings |

Several relations between the same two assets are folded into one line marked
**×n**.

**Point at a relation** to read it in plain words, such as *Lineage · data
flows payroll.xlsx → export.csv* or *Duplicate · 97% similar*, with a lock: the
platform's relations cannot be deleted from a board, because they are facts
about your data rather than opinions about the case. **Right-click** one for
**Why is this here?** (how it was found, and how sure the platform is) and
**Open both** (both assets, each in a new tab).

<Callout type="info">
Which kinds of relation the board draws is up to you: **View → Connections**
turns **Lineage**, **Links**, **Duplicates** and **Similar** on and off. The
same switches decide which relations lead to
[suggested neighbours](/investigations/cases/connections/#suggested-neighbours).
</Callout>

## Links you draw

A link is your own statement that two things belong together: two invoices that
contradict each other, a document that came before a payment, an email address
that is the same person as a vendor record. Links are amber, with their kind
written along them:

<LineKey only="drawn" />

### Drawing a link

<Steps>

### Point at the first thing

Four small **ports** appear around it, one on each side, a little away from
the node. Every asset, finding, hypothesis and note has them.

### Drag from any port to the other thing

The port under the pointer where the link would land lights up. Drop anywhere
on the other node, not only on a port. Lines always attach to the nearest side,
so it does not matter which port you started from.

### Say what the link means

A small menu opens where you dropped. Pick a kind: **Related to**, **Same
entity**, **Communicates with**, **Derived from**, **Contradicts**,
**Precedes**, or type your own with **Custom…**. The kinds you used recently
come first. Choose **Confirmed** or **Suspected**, add a label if you like, and press
**Link**. `Enter` takes the first kind; `Esc` cancels, and nothing is saved.

</Steps>

For many links in a row, switch on the **Link** tool (`L`). Every node then
becomes one big handle: drag from anywhere on it. You can link **a single
finding**, not only its asset: drag from the finding's own ports.

### Editing, promoting and deleting

Right-click a link:

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Edit link** | Change its kind or label, switch between **Confirmed** and **Suspected**, set its **confidence** (the line gets thicker the more confident it is) |
| **Promote to global relationship** | Copies the link into the shared graph of your data, where every case and the lineage view can see it. It gets a globe |
| **Delete link** | Removes it from this board. **Undo** brings it back |
| **Delete everywhere…** | For a global relationship: removes it for every case and the lineage view, after asking you to confirm |

Point at a link to see who drew it. You can also select a link, or a stance,
and press `Delete`; **Undo** brings it back.

## A hypothesis's stance

A stance says how a piece of evidence bears on a hypothesis: it **supports**
it, **contradicts** it, or is relevant without taking a side (**neutral**). The
glyph sits at the hypothesis end, so you can read the direction without a
legend:

<LineKey only="stances" />

Setting a stance:

- **Drag** from a hypothesis card to an asset or a finding (or the other way
  round). Instead of the kinds menu, three buttons ask **How does this bear on
  the hypothesis?** Click one and you are done.
- **Right-click** evidence → **Add to hypothesis** → pick the hypothesis and
  the stance. This works on several selected items at once.
- In the hypothesis's **thread**, link evidence from the **Evidence** tab. See
  [Hypotheses & threads](/investigations/cases/hypothesis/).

Right-click a stance line to **change the stance** or **remove** it. Point at it
to read the stance in words, with its confidence and note when it has them.

## When lines crowd

- Two lines between the same pair of things **bow apart** instead of drawing
  over each other.
- Zoomed out, the words along the lines are hidden so the picture stays
  readable; point at a line to read it anyway.
- Selecting something fades every line that does not touch it. Focusing a
  hypothesis (click its card) leaves only its stances.
