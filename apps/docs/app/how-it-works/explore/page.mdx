# Explore: Following Connections

After the first scans, you have assets, and whatever lineage, links and
duplicates came with them. Explore is where you follow them. It answers what a
list cannot:

- *Where does this table's data come from, and what is built on it?*
- *Is this the same document as that one, or does it only share a name with it?*
- *What else is this connected to, and is any of it already in a case?*
- *Nothing links this file yet: what is it like?*

Explore never draws your whole workspace. A picture of ten thousand documents is
a cloud of dots that answers nothing. It starts from **one asset**, a few, or a
source's most connected ones, and shows what is connected to them, one view at a
time. You meet it where you already are: the **Connections** tab of every source
and every asset, the overview of every glossary term, the **Explore** section of
duplicate review, and the Explore card on the Overview.

## On a source's page

A source's **Connections** tab opens on its **ten most connected assets**, and
what they are connected to: the lineage between its tables, the pages that link
its issues, the duplicates inside it and in other sources. The search in the top
bar searches this source. **Worth adding next** lists the neighbours reached by
the most kinds of connection.

The switch next to the counters says which connections to keep:

| Switch | Keeps |
|---|---|
| **All** | Every connection |
| **Same source** | Only connections between assets of this source |
| **Other sources** | Only connections that leave it: to another source's assets, or to objects no source has scanned yet |

An asset from another source carries that source's name under it, so a wiki
page linking a ticket reads as such at a glance. On the source's
page, *Other sources* starts from the assets with the most connections out of it.

## Diving in, and back out

**Double-click** an asset, or select it and press **Dive in**: the canvas
re-centres on it, with its own connections around it, traced the way you had
chosen. A trail at the top says where you are, *All of Service desk › HD-214 ›
HD-214 runbook*; click any step to go back out to it. **Open asset**
opens the asset's own page.

## On an asset's page

Every asset page has a **Connections** tab: the same canvas, starting from that
asset, with the same counters, switch, views, trace and panels, and the same way
of diving in. Select the asset to see its **meaning** (the concepts it is about,
by binding, declaration, mention or link by hand) and to link a concept by hand.
For a table or a view, the **column lineage** panel sits under the canvas.

## On a glossary term's page

A concept's or an entity's overview opens on **Where it is connected**: the ten
assets that carry it most (the most severe findings first, then the most
evidence), the term itself drawn between them and ringed, the other concepts
they share, and the documents those bring in. The term stays on the canvas in
every view, so switching to *Lineage* shows how the documents about it feed
each other without losing why they are there. Double-click a document to dive
in; the trail leads back to the term.

## In duplicate review

Duplicate review's **Explore** section draws its pairs on the canvas instead of
listing them: the documents in the most pairs are the starting points, each
with what is the same as it around it, every line with the reason the pair
screen gives (*same content*, *repeated text · 97%*, *likely the same · person
×2*, *confirmed the same*). Filters above the canvas pick which pairs:

| Filter | Picks |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | One [pattern](/duplicates/patterns/); a pattern's page links straight here with **Explore** |
| **Kind of match** | Shared values, similar spelling, identical content, repeated text |
| **Score** | Any, from the *worth a look* cutoff, or from the *likely the same* one |
| **Review** | Every pair not ruled apart, pairs not reviewed yet, or pairs confirmed the same |
| **Source** | Pairs with a document in one source |

The switch next to the counters keeps pairs **within one source** or **across
two**. Pairs someone ruled apart are never drawn as the same. Lineage and links
between the drawn documents come along, so a copy that is explained by lineage
shows it. Double-click a document to dive into everything it is connected to;
the queue is still where pairs are decided.

## Explore on the Overview

The Explore card on the [Overview](/how-it-works/in-the-app/#discovery) opens
Explore on its own page, for questions that start from nowhere in particular. It
opens on a search box, your **saved views**, and one table of assets worth
starting from, a tab per reason:

| Tab | Lists |
|---|---|
| **Results** | What the search found, by meaning *and* by the exact words, as on the [Assets](/sources/assets-and-metadata/) page, each with the passage that matched |
| **Most critical** | Assets with open critical and high findings, the most critical first |
| **Most lineage** | Assets with the most lineage links |
| **Duplicates** | The biggest [duplicate patterns](/duplicates/patterns/), each with its strongest pair |
| **Drawn links** | The links people drew last |

Press **Explore** on a row, or tick several and **Explore them together**.
Once something is on the canvas, the search moves to the top bar and answers in
the **Find** panel; **+** adds a result next to what is already there.

The view lives in the address bar, so **back** and **forward** walk your
exploration, and a copied link opens exactly what you were looking at.

## Saved views

When a canvas tells you something, keep it: **Save view** in the top bar gives it
a name and notes. A saved view keeps its starting points, the view, how far and
which way it traces, and **where you put things**: drag assets where they make
sense, and **Save changes** keeps the layout. The menu next to it saves a copy
as a new view, renames it, resets the layout, or deletes it.

Saved views belong to that page, and are listed on its start and on the Overview
card. They are kept
by the identity of the assets, so re-scans leave them whole; an asset deleted at
its source is not drawn, and comes back if the asset does.

## The views, and the guide

Explore shows one view at a time, and opens on the one your asset has most to
show in:

| View | Shows | Opens when |
|---|---|---|
| **Lineage** | Where the data comes from and where it goes, traced as far as you ask | The asset has any lineage |
| **Same and alike values** | The same content, likely the same thing, and shared detected values | It has duplicates or shares values |
| **All connected** | Lineage, links, duplicates and shared values together | It only has plain links |
| **By meaning** | The concepts and people it shares, and what reads alike | Nothing links it yet |

A line at the top of the canvas, the **guide**, says what the view shows (*Lineage:
7 upstream, 7 downstream*) and which other views have something for this asset
(*Also: By meaning 56*). One click switches.

An asset nothing links yet is not a dead end. Explore opens it **by meaning**, and
the guide says so: these are the documents it shares concepts with or reads like,
so you can link what belongs together.

## Tracing lineage

The **View** button in the bottom left corner chooses the view, and how lineage
and links are traced:

- **Upstream, Both, Downstream**: where the data came from, where it went, or both.
- **How far**: one step at a time up to **∞**, as far as the lineage goes.
  Duplicates and shared values go at most two steps; concepts and likeness are
  always one, because two steps of "alike" is no longer alike.
- **Kinds of connection**: switch individual kinds on or off when no view fits.

On the canvas, lineage reads left to right: what feeds your starting points stands
to their left, one column per step, what they feed stands to their right. A small
↑2 or ↓1 says how many steps away it is.

## Reading the canvas

The **counters** in the top bar count what is on the canvas, one per kind of
connection, each with the line it is drawn with. Press one to light that kind and
bring it into view; the rest steps back. Press it again to see everything.

- Your starting points are ringed.
- **Above** them: what is the *same* (duplicates) and what *shares values*.
- **Below** them: the concepts and entities they share, drawn as labelled boxes
  between the documents, then other documents about the same things, then what
  reads alike, then plain references.
- Every line carries its reason. Zoom in to read it on the line; point at a line
  to read it at any zoom. A fainter line is a weaker score; an amber line was
  drawn by a person.

| Kind | Means | The line says |
|---|---|---|
| **Lineage** | Data flowed from one into the other, reported by the systems themselves | *loaded into*, *transform*, *copy of* |
| **Links** | References between documents a scan found (a page that links an issue, a message and its attachment), usage, and links people drew | *references*, or what the person wrote |
| **Same as** | The same content, very likely the same thing, or the same text read passage for passage | *same content*, *likely the same · person ×2*, *repeated text · 97%* |
| **Shared values** | Both contain the same detected values: a person, an IBAN, an email | *shares person ×2, phone number ×1* |
| **Shared concepts** | Both are about the same [glossary](/how-it-works/glossary/) concept or entity | the concept itself, as a box |
| **Alike by meaning** | They read alike, compared by meaning rather than by words | *84% alike*, with the closest passage |

*Same as* and *shared values* come from the same evidence as
[duplicate review](/duplicates/): select one of those lines and **Review this
pair** opens it there. *Alike by meaning* needs
[embeddings](/settings/embeddings/) switched on.

## What is already in a case

An asset that is evidence in a [case](/investigations/cases/) wears a **CASE**
badge; one whose findings answer a [watch](/investigations/inquiry/) wears
**WATCH**. The top bar counts both, and the **Cases and watches** panel lists them
with links to each, so you see at once what is already being looked at and what
nobody has picked up yet.

## The panels

One rail on the right opens the panels, as on the case board:

| Panel | Holds |
|---|---|
| **Find** | Search results, and your starting points |
| **Worth adding next** | The neighbours reached by the most kinds of connection, the strongest first |
| **Details** | What you selected: an asset's links with their reasons and the cases and watches it is part of, a line's reason and score, a term's documents |
| **Cases and watches** | The cases holding these assets and the watches they answer |

On a source's or an asset's page, double-click an asset to dive in (see above).
On Explore's own page, a double click or **Add as a starting point** walks on
instead: its own connections come in around it, next to what is there, and
**Start over from this** makes it the only starting point. **All to a case**
sends everything on the canvas to a case as evidence; **Add to a case** sends
one asset.

## Your own links

You often know something the data does not: that the photos were taken for this
complaint, that this works order answers that survey. Select an asset and use
**Link to…**, or drag from the edge of one asset to another, and say what the
link means:

| Meaning | Where it shows |
|---|---|
| **Related to** | As a plain link, no direction |
| **Same as** | With the duplicates |
| **Feeds** | As lineage, in the [lineage view](/sources/lineage/) too |
| **References** | As a link, one way |
| **Something else** | As a link, named by you |

Add a note saying why. Links people drew are amber everywhere, so they are never
mistaken for something a system reported.

<Callout type="info">
**What happens to your link later.** A link is kept by the identity of the two
documents, not by a copy of them. Re-scanning either source does not touch it,
and neither does recomputing duplicates. If one of the documents is deleted at
its source, the link disappears from view, and comes back if the document does.
Deleting a source removes the links of its documents with it.
</Callout>

## Built to stay fast

Explore never reads the whole workspace. Each kind of connection is read
separately and with its own limit, so a document with a thousand look-alikes
cannot crowd out its lineage, and a workspace of millions of documents answers as
quickly as one of hundreds. When a limit is reached, the guide says so; add a
neighbour as a starting point to see its own connections.

## For AI agents

The same view is available to agents over [MCP](/settings/mcp-server/):
`explore_connections` returns what assets are connected to and why, traced as far
and in the direction asked, with the cases and watches each asset is part of; given
a source instead of assets, it starts from the source's most connected ones, and it
keeps all connections, those inside the source or those leaving it;
`find_similar_assets` returns the assets that read like a given one.
